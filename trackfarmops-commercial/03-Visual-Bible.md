# TRACKFARMOPS — VISUAL BIBLE
"The Land Remembers" — 90-Second Commercial

The visual language of this film has one job: make the audience **feel** the weight of Act 1 and **feel** the release of Act 2 and 3, purely through light, color, and camera behavior. Dialogue is absent. Every visual choice below is doing narrative work.

---

## 1. THE CORE VISUAL PRINCIPLE

> "Just elegant, understated intelligence woven into the landscape. No clutter. No giant holograms."

This is an Apple-grade restraint brief, applied to an African agricultural story. Every frame should be able to survive as a single still image in a print campaign. If a shot needs explaining, it's too busy.

---

## 2. COLOR SCRIPT

### Act 1 — THE WEIGHT (0:00–0:25)
| Element | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Overall grade | Desaturated, cool-neutral. Blue-grey shadows, muted earth midtones. Think pre-dawn film stock — Malick's *Days of Heaven* by way of a phone commercial. |
| Sky | Overcast or misty dawn — never a clean, warm sunrise. Weight comes from ambiguous light. |
| Skin tones | Slightly cool, slightly underexposed — never sickly, just unresolved. |
| Notebooks | Warm browns, but dulled — no visual life in the props themselves. |
| Accent color | **None.** Act 1 has zero saturated color. This is the contrast bank we spend in Act 2. |

### Act 2 — THE TRANSFORMATION (0:25–1:10)
| Element | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Trigger | The tablet powers on at 00:25 — this is the single moment color enters the film. Treat it like a lens flare event: everything before is cool/grey, everything after gradually warms and saturates. |
| Brand accent | **Emerald green** (`#10b981` / Tailwind `emerald-500`–`emerald-600`) as the singular "intelligence" color — glowing UI elements, digital tags, dashboard pulses. This must be the ONLY saturated green in the frame; foliage and grass stay naturalistic so the UI glow reads as distinctly digital. |
| Progression | Each of the 5 beats (Income/Expenses → Inventory → Assets → Livestock → Analytics/Ecosystem) should incrementally raise overall scene saturation and warmth by roughly 5–8%, cumulative. By the control room scene, the image should feel fully "alive" compared to Act 1. |
| Practical light sources | Tablet/screen glow should be a true practical light source in every beat — cast real green-white light onto faces and surrounding surfaces, not composited on top. |

### Act 3 — THE INSPIRATION (1:10–1:30)
| Element | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Overall grade | Golden hour, warm and full. This is the payoff of the entire saturation build. |
| Sky | Clear, sun-flared, wide open — the visual opposite of the Act 1 mist. |
| Final frames | Pure black background for logo card — let the emerald-to-gold brand gradient be the only light in frame. |

---

## 3. LIGHTING PHILOSOPHY

- **Act 1:** Flat, directionless, slightly overcast — light with no clear source mirrors a man with no clear answers.
- **Act 2:** Motivated practical light from screens/tags is introduced deliberately — technology becomes a literal light source in the farmer's world. Every "reveal" beat (notebook dissolving to ledger, tag illuminating on a sack, tag illuminating on tractor) is lit as a small, contained hero light — like a match being struck.
- **Act 3:** Full, motivated, natural sunlight — no more artificial glow needed because the farmer no longer needs his tools to be visibly special; clarity has become ambient, not exceptional.
- **Control room (01:02–01:10):** Glass-walled, so exterior farm light bleeds in alongside interior display glow — visually merging "the field" and "the intelligence" in one frame, foreshadowing the ecosystem cross-cut.

---

## 4. CAMERA LANGUAGE

| Act | Movement style | Lens feel | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Act 1 | Slow, heavy, slightly handheld drift. Drone pull-back is the one exception — big and slow, establishing scale/burden. | Longer focal lengths, some compression, slightly desaturated vintage glass feel. | Weight and isolation. |
| Act 2 | Camera becomes steadier and more confident with each beat. Consider a very subtle, cumulative shift from handheld to stabilized/gimbal across the 5 beats — the camera itself "gets organized." | Cleaner, more modern glass character; wider apertures for the glowing tag/screen macro inserts. | The camera's own confidence mirrors the farmer's arc — this is a directing trick worth protecting in the edit. |
| Act 3 | Full stabilized/drone, slow motion (partially specified in script — "slow motion" at 01:10). Final ascending drone shot is the emotional and technical climax of the film. | Wide, epic glass. Anamorphic flares acceptable on sun position. | Release, scale, inspiration. |

### The Final Ascending Shot (01:18–01:30)
This is the single most expensive and important shot in the film. Treat it as its own mini-production:
- Continuous (or seamlessly stitched) drone ascent from human eye-level to extreme high altitude.
- Must reveal, in order: the farmer's own farm → neighboring farms → varied landscape types (crop, livestock, warehouse, roads) → an implied national/continental scale.
- "Glowing lines" connecting locations should be added in post as a restrained, thin emerald line-art overlay — this is the only "hologram-adjacent" effect permitted in the entire film, and it must be minimal, more infographic than sci-fi.
- See `Prompts/Veo-Prompts.md` for AI pre-visualization prompt language for this shot.

## 5. THE NOTEBOOK-TO-LIGHT TRANSFORMATION (Signature VFX Beat)

This effect appears 4 times (Income/Expenses, Inventory, Assets, Livestock) plus once conceptually at the control room. Consistency here is the visual backbone of Act 2.

**Transformation recipe (must be identical each time):**
1. Object (notebook page / grain sack / tractor hood / animal ear tag) catches a single point of light.
2. Light spreads along the object's edges in a thin emerald line — like a circuit tracing itself.
3. Object's surface briefly turns semi-transparent / glass-like as the "dissolve" occurs.
4. Digital information (numbers, tags, stats) resolves out of the light, sharp and legible, hovering just above or on the surface of the real object — never floating disconnected in space.
5. Real object returns to full opacity, now with a subtle permanent glow line indicating it is "connected."

**Do not vary this recipe.** The repetition is intentional — see script production notes: *"Each transformation moment should use the same dissolve effect, building a visual rhythm the eye learns to anticipate."*

## 6. COMPOSITION RULES

- **Act 1:** Off-center, negative-space-heavy framing. The farmer is frequently small in frame relative to the land — he is dwarfed by the scale of what he's responsible for.
- **Act 2:** Framing tightens and centers progressively. By the control room scene, the farmer is centered, framed symmetrically against the glass wall.
- **Act 3:** Wide, symmetrical, classical "hero" framing — rule-of-thirds placement on the ridge shot, dead-center for the final logo card.
- **UI overlays:** Never placed over faces. Always anchored to objects (tractors, sacks, ear tags, screens) or floating in clearly "digital" negative space (control room glass, tablet screen). See `04-UI-Bible.md` for exact placement and style rules.

---

## 7. TEXTURE & GRAIN

- Subtle 35mm film grain throughout — even in "digital glow" moments — to keep the film feeling premium and cinematic rather than like a software demo video.
- Notebooks: tactile, high-frequency detail (paper fiber, water stains, torn edges) — shot in macro where possible to sell the "weight" of analog record-keeping.
- Digital UI: crisp, anti-aliased, zero grain on the glow itself — deliberate contrast between "analog texture" and "digital clarity" reinforces the film's thesis visually.

---

## 8. REFERENCE TOUCHSTONES

- **Apple "Shot on iPhone" campaigns** — for the restraint of UI-in-environment compositing (small, contextual, never overwhelming).
- **Tesla product films** — for the confident, unhurried camera movement in Act 2/3.
- **Nike "Find Your Greatness"** — for the emotional authenticity of real bodies, real labor, understated heroism.
- **Terrence Malick (*Days of Heaven*, *The Tree of Life*)** — for Act 1's naturalistic, magic-hour-adjacent but weighted cinematography.
- **John Lewis Christmas ads** — for the single-character emotional through-line carried without dialogue.

---

## 9. WHAT TO AVOID

- No neon or cyberpunk color grading on the UI glow — emerald green must read as *organic technology*, not sci-fi.
- No floating 3D holograms detached from physical objects.
- No lens flares that look stock/generic — flares should feel motivated by the sun or a specific screen light source.
- No stylized slow-motion in Act 1 (that's reserved for the emotional release in Act 3 — using it early dilutes the payoff).
- No saturated colors competing with the emerald brand accent in Act 2 — keep secondary elements (worker vests, vehicles) muted so the UI glow is always the most saturated thing in frame.
