# TRACKFARMOPS — CHARACTER BIBLE
"The Land Remembers" — 90-Second Commercial

This document defines every human presence on screen. The rule governing all casting: **authenticity over polish**. Real trades, real hands, real settings. No character should look like a stock-photo farmer. Every character is a stand-in for a real TrackFarmOps user persona — cast and direct them accordingly.

---

## 1. THE FARMER (Lead / "Our Man")

**Screen time:** ~65% of the film. The emotional spine.

| Attribute | Direction |
|---|---|
| Age | Late 40s–early 60s. Enough years on the land to have earned the weathering, not enough to read as retired. |
| Build | Lean, functional strength — not gym-built. Farm-labor physique. |
| Hands | **Critical.** Cracked knuckles, dirt under nails, a scar or two. Hands do more acting than the face in this film. |
| Wardrobe (Act 1) | Muted earth tones — faded canvas jacket, rolled sleeves, worn boots caked in dry mud. The satchel strap has visibly worn a groove into the shoulder fabric over years. |
| Wardrobe (Act 2 & 3) | Same wardrobe, unchanged — the transformation is in his environment and posture, never his clothes. This is intentional: the technology elevates the man, it doesn't costume him. |
| Physicality (Act 1) | Weighted walk. Shoulders pulled slightly forward by the satchel. Eyes down, scanning the ground rather than the horizon — a man managing problems, not vision. |
| Physicality (Act 2) | Posture straightens incrementally with each transformation beat (notebook → light). By the control room scene he stands fully upright. |
| Physicality (Act 3) | Shoulders back, satchel empty and folded — weightless. Eyes now on the horizon, not the ground. This is the entire arc in one physical detail. |
| Face | Not conventionally "commercial handsome." Real texture — sun lines, a face that has made decisions in bad weather. Trustworthy, unbothered by camera. |
| Casting note | Prioritize actual farmers or agricultural extension workers over trained actors if performance range allows. If using an actor, cast someone with visible manual-labor experience (calloused hands are non-negotiable — no glove work-arounds in close-up). |
| Voice (if he speaks) | He does not speak on camera. All narration is V.O. His performance is entirely physical and facial. |

**Do not:** give him a smile until 01:10. His arc is earned in silence.

## 2. THE ECOSYSTEM CAST (01:02–01:10 cross-cut sequence)

Eight roles, roughly 1 second of screen time each, unified by consistent lighting and UI so the sequence reads as one connected system, not a stitched-together montage. Each character maps directly to a TrackFarmOps user role.

### 2.1 Farm Owner
- Mirrors "The Farmer" in wardrobe register but slightly more composed — this may be a different owner on a different farm, establishing scale.
- Viewing the dashboard on a tablet from a farmhouse porch.
- Expression: quiet confidence, not surprise. He trusts the number because he's seen it every day.

### 2.2 Farm Manager / Field Supervisor
- Younger, mid-30s. Standing in a field with workers visible in soft focus behind them.
- Wardrobe: branded cap or hi-vis vest — signals operational, boots-on-ground authority.
- Holding a phone, not a tablet — manager persona is mobile-first, moving between locations.

### 2.3 Accountant / Bookkeeper
- Indoor office setting, small but tidy. Desktop monitor + the mobile app open beside it.
- Wardrobe: business-casual, short-sleeve shirt, reading glasses pushed up on forehead.
- Reaction: a small, satisfied nod at a reconciled number — the "down to the coin" payoff from Act 2.

### 2.4 Warehouse Officer
- Same figure introduced in the Inventory beat (00:38–00:46) — reappears here for continuity, now wearing a headset/radio, cross-checking a shipment against the tablet.
- Hi-vis vest, warehouse gloves, clipboard replaced by tablet (visual metaphor of transition).

### 2.5 Veterinarian
- Same figure from the Livestock Health beat (00:54–01:02) — female, confident, kneeling posture reused for continuity.
- Wardrobe: vet coveralls, stethoscope, mud-appropriate boots.

### 2.6 Investor
- Lagos boardroom, floor-to-ceiling windows, city skyline visible.
- Wardrobe: tailored suit, no tie (modern African business casual) — signals sophistication without stiffness.
- Viewing the dashboard on a large boardroom display or tablet — same UI, different context. This juxtaposition (mud-caked boots ↔ marble boardroom, same dashboard) is the thesis of the whole ecosystem beat.

### 2.7 Cooperative Leader
- Outdoors, under a tree — deliberate visual echo of the "cooperative meeting" frustration scene in Act 1 (00:16–00:25). Same location if possible, transformed from chaos to clarity.
- Wardrobe: traditional dress with modern touches (smartphone, not a ledger).
- Surrounded by 2–3 other cooperative members glancing at a shared screen — communal, not solitary, viewing.

### 2.8 Government / Agricultural Analyst
- Office setting, subtle national agricultural branding in the background (a wall map of the region, not overtly governmental logos to avoid dating the film).
- Wardrobe: formal, understated.
- Viewing aggregated, anonymized regional data — this shot should feel like "zooming out" thematically, previewing the final aerial pull-back of Act 3.

**Unifying direction for all 8:** Same warm color temperature, same UI chrome (emerald accent glow, glass-morphic card style — see `04-UI-Bible.md`), same "quiet confidence" emotional register. No one is amazed. Everyone is simply informed. That restraint is the brand's maturity signal.

## 3. BACKGROUND / ATMOSPHERE CHARACTERS

| Scene | Characters | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Act 1 cooperative meeting (00:16–00:25) | 4–6 farmers around mismatched ledgers | Frustrated, talking over each other, real texture — sun-worn faces, disagreement body language |
| Act 1 investor scene | 1 investor, unconvinced | Closing a folder mid-pitch — a small, cold gesture. No dialogue needed, the folder-close tells the story |
| Act 1 government map scene | 1–2 analysts | Studying hand-written pins on a physical map, mild confusion/friction |
| Act 2 mechanic (Assets beat) | 1 mechanic | Practiced, unhurried hand movement along tractor hood — a person who has done this a thousand times, now doing it smarter |
| Act 2 control room | Silhouettes/soft-focus figures | Optional — keep the room feeling alive without pulling focus from the farmer |
| Act 3 final aerial | No visible individuals | Pure landscape — the human story has already been told; scale takes over |

## 4. CASTING PRIORITIES (in order)

1. **Hands and posture over face** — this is a film about physical weight and release, not dialogue delivery.
2. **Real trade authenticity** for vet, mechanic, warehouse officer — hire from the actual professions if budget allows, even for a day.
3. **Regional accuracy** — cast reflects Nigerian/West African agricultural demographics unless the brief specifies a broader pan-African or global cut.
4. **Age and gender diversity across the ecosystem cast** — the 8-role sequence should visibly represent a cross-section: owner, manager, accountant, warehouse, vet, investor, cooperative, government — not skew toward a single demographic.
5. **No hero actor recognizable from other ads** — protects the authenticity read.

## 5. WARDROBE CONTINUITY RULES

- The Farmer's wardrobe **never changes** across all three acts — see `09-Continuity-Bible.md` for the full continuity matrix.
- The satchel is the single most important continuity prop tied to a character. Track its exact wear pattern, strap position, and buckle state across every setup — see `08-Asset-Tracker.md`.
- All ecosystem-cast wardrobe should avoid brand logos other than TrackFarmOps (no visible competing tech, generic agricultural brands only where necessary for realism).
