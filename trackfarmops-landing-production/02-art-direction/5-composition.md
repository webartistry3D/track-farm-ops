---

# Philosophy

Composition tells the story before animation begins.

Every frame should have one unmistakable focal point.

If the visitor's eyes hesitate, the composition has failed.

---

# Cinematic Principle

Design every scene as though it could be paused and printed as a film still.

Every frame should communicate meaning without motion.

---

# Primary Composition Rule

One Hero.

One Supporting Element.

One Background.

Nothing else competes.

---

# Rule of Thirds

Primary subjects should align naturally with thirds.

Avoid placing every subject in the exact center.

Exception:

Final CTA.

The logo and call-to-action should be perfectly centered to communicate confidence and completion.

---

# Negative Space

Negative space is an active design element.

It provides:

Breathing room

Visual clarity

Focus

Premium perception

Never fill empty areas simply because they exist.

---

# Reading Direction

Design for left-to-right reading.

Preferred eye path:

Hero Element

↓

Supporting Motion

↓

Headline

↓

Dashboard

↓

CTA

Every composition should support this natural flow.

# Visual Weight

Large objects attract attention.

Bright objects attract attention.

Moving objects attract attention.

Use these intentionally.

Never compete with the hero element.

---

# Layer Hierarchy

Layer 1

Background

Layer 2

Environment

Layer 3

Characters

Layer 4

Documents

Layer 5

Dashboard

Layer 6

Typography

Layer 7

Navigation

Maintain this order consistently unless a scene explicitly requires otherwise.

---

# Depth

Depth should come from:

Perspective

Scale

Layer separation

Parallax

Natural overlap

Avoid artificial 3D tricks.

---

# Symmetry

Use asymmetry for storytelling.

Use symmetry for resolution.

Examples:

Chaos

Asymmetrical.

Order

Balanced.

CTA

Perfectly symmetrical.

The composition itself reinforces the narrative.

---

# Framing

Leave comfortable margins around primary objects.

Avoid cropping important storytelling elements.

Characters should never feel trapped inside the frame.

---

# Motion Composition

Objects should move toward areas of visual importance.

Examples:

Documents orbit inward.

Charts grow upward.

Maps expand outward.

Dashboard assembles centrally.

Movement should strengthen composition, not fight it.

---

# Camera Framing

Preferred shots:

Wide Establishing

Medium Product

Close Detail

Avoid excessive close-ups.

The visitor should always understand the spatial context.

---

# Mobile Composition

Do not simply shrink desktop layouts.

Recompose scenes.

Maintain the same hierarchy while simplifying visual density.

Every mobile frame should remain readable and emotionally effective.

---

# Composition Checklist

Before approving any scene, verify:

✓ Clear focal point

✓ Strong hierarchy

✓ Comfortable negative space

✓ Balanced visual weight

✓ Logical eye movement

✓ Consistent framing

✓ Narrative supported by composition

---

# Final Principle

Every composition should answer one question:

"Where should the visitor look first?"

If the answer is anything other than the intended story element, the composition must be refined.
